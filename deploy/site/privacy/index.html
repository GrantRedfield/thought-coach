Privacy and fair play

Thought Coach is a free, open-source tool that reviews how you think in your own lichess games. This page says what it collects, why, how to delete it, and how it stays fair. Last updated 27 November 2026.

Fair play: nothing during the game

  • No move suggestions, no evaluations, no hints, no coaching while a game is being played. During a game the extension shows nothing on the lichess page; once the game is over, a small panel asks whether to send it for review.
  • It only records what you do (arrows, circles, piece pickups, and your voice if switched on). Nothing is analysed until the game is over.
  • The recording is sent for review only after lichess reports the game finished; the engine runs on our servers after that.
  • Thought Coach is not affiliated with, approved or endorsed by lichess. As with any tool, follow lichess's own fair-play rules.

What the browser extension records

  • Only on lichess.org game pages, and nothing on other websites. Only games you played are reviewed (thoughtcoach.org refuses any other game). On thoughtcoach.org the extension only connects itself to your account.
  • The arrows and circles you draw on the board, when you pick up and put down pieces, the positions, and the times these happened.
  • Your voice, if you allow it. Only while you are playing a game (never when watching one), the microphone is recorded so you can think out loud. You can switch this off in the extension at any time; your browser also asks your permission first.

Nothing is shown to you or sent anywhere during a game. When lichess says the game is over, the recording is sent to thoughtcoach.org for review, and the voice audio is removed from your browser.

What the website stores

  • Your lichess username and title (if you have one), from signing in with lichess. We don't see your password, and we don't keep access to your lichess account: we give the sign-in back to lichess straight away.
  • An upload key that links the extension to your account.
  • Your recordings and the reviews made from them. Voice audio is deleted once it has been transcribed; the text transcript is kept as part of the review.
  • Puzzles, if you try them signed in: your puzzle rating and which puzzles you tried, with the move you chose. Every puzzle also keeps its own rating, worked out from everyone's tries, with no names. Your lichess puzzle rating (public on your profile) is read once, to choose where your rating starts.
  • A sign-in cookie, used only to keep you signed in. No advertising or tracking cookies.
  • Anonymous step counts: the public pages count how many times a few steps happen each day (the home page was opened, the sample review was opened, the setup page was reached). Only a daily total per step is stored: no cookie, no user id, no IP address, nothing from your games. No third-party analytics service is used.

How it is used

To make your reviews: the game is analysed with the Stockfish chess engine, and the voice is turned into text and tagged by speech models running on our own servers. Two more uses are described below: anonymous puzzles from players' games, and, for titled players, the pooled master benchmarks. Your data is not sold, not used for advertising, not used to train AI models, and not shared with anyone else, except the services that run the site:

  • lichess.org: for signing in, and to check that a game is over and that you played in it (public game data).
  • Amazon Web Services (United States): hosting, storage, and the servers that run the engine and the speech-to-text models.
  • Google, only if you install from the Chrome Web Store: the store handles the install, as for any extension.

Titled players and the master benchmarks

If your lichess account has a title (GM, IM, FM, CM, NM, LM and the women's titles; never bot accounts), your rapid and classical reviews are included in the master benchmarks that every player is compared with. Only pooled rates are published, averaged with other strong players: you are never named, quoted or shown individually, and no game or transcript of yours is shown to anyone else (positions may appear as anonymous puzzles, as below, unless you switch that off). Your title is read from lichess when you sign in (it is public on your lichess profile). You can switch this off at any time on the setup page; your games then drop out the next time the benchmarks are rebuilt, as they do if you delete your data.

Puzzles from players' games

Positions from reviewed games can become puzzles for other players: a moment where one move was clearly best. A puzzle is anonymous:

  • Shown: the position, the clock, the engine's answer and line, and a rating range for the player (for example "1900–2000 rapid"), credited as "a Thought Coach player's game".
  • Never shown: your username, a link to the game or its date, your arrows, your words or your voice. What players drew and said may only feed pooled figures across many players (for example, how often a kind of threat was missed), never shown as one person's.
  • Which games: any game you review, unless you switch this off on the setup page.
  • How anonymous: we never attach your name, but a lichess game is public, so someone could look up an exact position in public game databases and find the game it came from. If that matters to you, switch it off.
  • Your choice: switch it off on the setup page at any time. Your positions then leave the puzzles the next time the set is rebuilt, as they do if you delete your data.

Puzzles from the games of named masters (such as the current set) are shown only with that player's permission.

Who can see your reviews

Your reviews are private: your games page, every review and story, and the live status of a review are shown only to you when you're signed in. The server checks who is asking before it sends a page; knowing or guessing your username or a game's address isn't enough.

  • Share links. From your games page or a review, Share makes a link to one game or to all your reviews. Anyone with that link can open what it covers. You can revoke a link at any time, and it stops working straight away, even for people who already opened it.
  • Public, if you choose. You can make all your reviews public instead. Then anyone with your page's address can open them. You can switch back to private at any time.
  • Search engines are asked not to index these pages either way. Your voice audio is never on them; what you said appears as text in the story and review.
  • If you'd rather nothing stayed online, delete your data at any time: that removes your reviews and your share links too.

How long it is kept

Voice audio: until it is transcribed (minutes). Everything else: until you delete it.

Deleting your data

Sign in, open Setup and choose Delete my data: your account, recordings, reviews and pages are deleted straight away. Uninstalling the extension deletes everything it keeps in your browser.

Open source

The extension and the server are open source, so you can check every line of what they do: https://github.com/GrantRedfield/thought-coach.
