Get set up

Three quick steps (about two minutes), then play. This page ticks each one off as you go.

  1. Sign in with lichess

    So your reviews are yours. Lichess only tells us your username: we never see your password and get no access to your account.

    Sign in with lichess

  2. Add the extension to Chrome Installed

    It records your arrows, piece pickups and voice on lichess, and sends the game here when it's over. Works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers on a computer.

    Add to Chrome Free, from the Chrome Web Store. Then come back to this tab: it ticks itself off.
    Install by hand instead
    1. Download the extension then unzip it (right-click the file → Extract All).
    2. Open in a new tab (paste it into the address bar; browsers don't allow links to it).
    3. Switch on Developer mode (top right).
    4. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped thoughtcoach-extension folder.
    5. Come back here: this step ticks itself off (or check again).

    The extension is open source: you can read every line it runs.

  3. Connect it to your account Connected

    Happens by itself once you're signed in and the extension is installed.

  4. Your voice On

    Voice is on by default: thinking out loud is the richest thing you can give the review (the lines you calculate, what you worry about, how you feel). Chrome asks for your microphone the first time, and you can switch it off here at any time; arrows alone work too. The audio is deleted once it's turned into text.

    Switch it on or off in the extension: click the Thought Coach icon in Chrome's toolbar (under the puzzle piece) and tick or untick Record my voice while I play.

    You can switch it on or off here once the extension is installed, or any time from the extension's icon.

  5. Play a game

    Play a rapid or classical game on lichess (10+0 or slower) as you normally would. Nothing appears during the game. The more you draw and say, the more the review can tell you: how to get the most out of your first games.

    Ask the four questionsTheir threat, the weaknesses, your two or three candidates, the piece doing least: the order strong players use. Answer them with arrows, circles or out loud. What to ask
    Think out loudSay the lines you calculate, what you think of the position, how you feel. If voice is on, Chrome asks for the microphone at the first game: allow it.
    Then wait a few minutesYour review is ready about five minutes after the game ends. You'll get a notification.
