Your first games

Thought Coach can only review the thinking it can see. Every arrow you draw and everything you say during a game gives it something to check. A game with no arrows and no words can only be judged on its moves, which lichess already does.

  1. 01Draw your ideas

    Right-click and drag to draw an arrow for each move you think about. Right-click a square to circle it. Left-click anywhere on the board clears them. Your opponent never sees your arrows.

    • from your piecea move you're considering
    • from their piecewhat they want to play
    • circlea weak square, a target, a piece in trouble

  2. 02Think out loud (optional)

    Talk the way a streamer does. Half sentences are fine and nothing is too small. Say the lines you calculate, your opinion of the position and how you feel. Feelings count too: the review looks at how your mood and your moves go together. Voice is optional, but it's the richest thing you can give the review: the more you say, the more precisely it finds where your thinking goes wrong. Switch it on or off on the setup page or from the Thought Coach icon in Chrome.

    • a line"if knight takes e5, queen d4 forks"
    • an opinion"my bishop is terrible here"
    • their idea"he wants to push f5"
    • a feeling"I'm nervous about my king"
    • a verdict"this is probably lost"
    • being stuck"no idea what to do"

  3. 03Ask the four questions

    What does my opponent want? Where are the weaknesses? What are my two or three candidates? Which piece is doing least? That's the order strong players work in. Arrows and words are how you answer them. What each question means

Expect a few awkward games

Drawing and talking while you play feels strange at first. You'll be slower, and you may play a bit worse for a few games. That's normal, and it passes quickly. Build it up one habit at a time:

  1. Game 1Only draw their threat after each of their moves.
  2. Game 2Add an arrow for every move of yours you consider.
  3. Game 3Start talking: one sentence a move is plenty.
  4. Game 4 onAll of it, and the full four questions when the position changes character: that's where strong players spend their time.

Make it easier on yourself

  • Play slower games. 10+5 or 15+10 rapid leaves time to draw and talk. Bullet and fast blitz don't.
  • Start with casual games, or against the lichess computer, so your rating isn't on the line while you get used to it.
  • Use a headset or a quiet room so the speech model hears you clearly. Speak at normal volume, in English.
  • Don't clean up your thinking. Bad ideas, doubts and gut feelings are just as useful to the review as good ones.
  • In time trouble, just play. Moves made with under 30 seconds left aren't judged on your thinking.

Fair play. Nothing is shown to you during the game. Arrows and talking are only your own notes; the review happens after lichess says the game is over.
